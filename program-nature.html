Program Pillar

Nature Protection & Biodiversity Conservation

Protecting and restoring the ecosystems of the Congo Basin around Virunga National Park through community-led conservation — because the people living alongside this landscape are its most consistent guardians.

The challenge

Conservation only holds if the neighbouring community can afford it

Displacement and poverty push households toward charcoal production and forest-edge clearing simply to cook a meal or earn an income. Top-down enforcement alone rarely changes that calculation. ACV's conservation work pairs protection with real alternatives, so that keeping the forest standing is also the more practical choice.

ACV Nature & Biodiversity Programme
How we work

What this program actually does

Each activity below is designed to reinforce at least one other ACV pillar — nothing here runs in isolation.

Tree nurseries and school-ground planting that restore degraded land in stages, prioritising native species and areas most exposed to erosion and encroachment.
Trained community patrols and simple mapping tools track encroachment, illegal felling and wildlife pressure around the park boundary, feeding early warnings to local authorities and park partners.
Practical ecology lessons paired with the Miti Alliance programme so conservation is something pupils practise, not just read about.
Promotion of improved cookstoves and alternative fuel briquettes that cut household firewood and charcoal demand without cutting cooking capacity.

Part of a bigger model

Conservation works best paired with food security and livelihoods

Reducing pressure on forests only sticks when households have another way to eat and earn — which is why this pillar is designed to run alongside agriculture and livelihoods, not instead of them.
